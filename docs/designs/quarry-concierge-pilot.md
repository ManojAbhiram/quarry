## Problem Statement
Quarry is an AI-powered B2B lead generation product (FastAPI + SQLite backend, React frontend). The founder wants it to become a real startup that meets business standards. The product works end to end and a flow audit (`reports/Quarry lead flow business audit.md`) found the flow shaped correctly, with its "fix now" security and failure-visibility items already implemented. What is missing is any evidence that a specific person wants it.

## Demand Evidence
None yet. Founder's words: "I haven't talked to anyone." No named user, no paying customer, no one who has used it. All current features are guesses about what a buyer wants.

## Status Quo
Unknown. Founder: "I don't know yet." Market research (vendor-authored, directional) puts the incumbent baseline at an entry-level outbound stack under $120/month and Apollo from $49/month. The most-cited incumbent complaint is data accuracy and staleness, with an unaudited claim that accuracy is much lower outside North America. The real status quo for the target user has not been observed.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
Target user: not yet named. Working hypothesis (premise 1): fresh, accurate contacts for one niche where the large databases are stale or thin, found live from the web per ICP. Narrowest wedge: a lead list run through Quarry by hand for 3 named people in that niche, delivered as a CSV.

## Constraints
- Free to build: free tiers and free tools only (founder: "free is mentioned to build the application").
- In the live product, users bring their own Serper key, so search cost lands on the user. In the concierge pilot the founder runs the pipeline, so the founder's key pays; the free-tier query limit is unverified (CEO review C1).
- Real third-party contact data triggers the open legal question from the audit, so the pilot stays small and per-customer.
- Pilot privacy minimum (CEO review R3, not legal advice): each CSV carries a one-line source notice and how to ask for removal; keep a removal list and check it before every delivery; delete all pilot lists and contacts 30 days after delivery; keep pilot people out of any shared table or database.
- Solo builder; goal is a real startup following business standards.

## Premises
1. The problem to solve is fresh, accurate contacts for a specific niche, not "more leads". Agreed.
2. Stop adding features until 5 to 10 conversations are done. Agreed.
3. Decide "per-customer tool" versus "shared database" before collecting more data; default to per-customer processing. Agreed.
4. Bring-your-own-keys plus free tiers is a fine build and pilot cost model, not yet a business model. Agreed.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Conversations only
Minimal. Interviews with 5-10 lead-list builders, no code. Not chosen alone: yields opinions, not proof; folded into C as step one.
### Approach B: Close the audit's "next" tier, then open a public pilot
Ideal architecture (suppression list, durable job queue, honest email labels, sample run, privacy notice). Rejected for now: hardens a product with zero customers and delays real evidence.
### Approach C: Concierge pilot in one niche (chosen)
Pick one niche, run Quarry by hand for 3 named people, hand over a CSV, measure bounce rate, replies and whether they ask for more.

## Recommended Approach
C. It produces the named customer evidence that is missing, tests demand and data accuracy together, and reuses the existing pipeline and export. Estimated effort: human ~2 weeks / CC ~30 min to script the run and a bounce-tracking sheet. Start with A's conversations.

UI direction (chosen: C · The Sheet): the pilot screens are the request wizard and the results view only. The look follows the spreadsheet pilot users already work in: bordered grid, numbers first, green accent, flat and dense. Email status is labelled Verified, Likely or Risky with a checked-at date, source and "why it matched" per lead, plus a cost and time estimate before launch and visible running, empty and failed states. The unfinished sequencing button stays disabled and labelled "coming soon". Rejected: A · Clean Ledger (calm, brand cyan; the fallback if the product outgrows the spreadsheet frame) and B · Control Room (dark, dense; speaks to tool-heavy users, no evidence yet that these are the pilot users). Mockup: `projects/Lead-Generation/designs/mockup-20261108/quarry-ui-directions.html` (state-root relative). Free-tier image generation was unavailable, so this is hand-built HTML, not generated images. The choice is a guess until real users react, and building it follows the conversations, not before. UI requirements added by /autoplan (R5, auto-decided): the results view must also show a partial-results state (a run that fails midway keeps and labels the leads found so far, with the failure reason), and the build must define mobile and narrow-width behavior (collapse the grid to a stacked list) and check accent and status-label contrast before launch. The mockup is desktop-only at 1440px.

## Open Questions
- Which niche? (Needs the conversations to answer.)
- Who is the actual buyer: agency owner, freelance SDR, founder doing outbound?
- Per-customer tool or shared database? (Business and legal decision; defaults to per-customer.)
- How will it eventually earn money? (Open; free-to-build is settled, revenue is not.)
- Is real contact data in a 3-person pilot acceptable without a privacy notice and opt-out? Needs a minimum notice and a legal check.

## Success Criteria
- At least 5 conversations held with people who build B2B lead lists, each with their current workflow and cost written down.
- 3 named people receive a Quarry-generated list in one niche.
- Measured (CEO review R2): before delivery, run an independent email verification pass on each list and record the result. Seven days after delivery, ask the pilot user three questions (emails sent, bounces, replies) and log the answers in a sheet. Quarry cannot see bounces or replies itself.
- Commitment ask (CEO review R1): after delivering a list, ask each of the 3 users to do one costly thing: pay a small amount, introduce a colleague who would use it, or send the list in a live campaign within a week. Asking for a second list does not count as demand.
- Time box (CEO review R1): the pilot closes 3 weeks after the first delivery with a written go/no-go.
- Decision rule: if none of the 3 meets the commitment ask by the time box, the niche or the product thesis is wrong; revisit premise 1 before writing more code.

## Distribution Plan
Existing web service. No new distribution work for the pilot; lists are delivered by hand as CSV.

## Dependencies
- A Serper key and an LLM free-tier key (already in the setup).
- Reaching 5-10 people in the target niche (founder's network or outreach).
- Legal input on the data-broker question before any shared or retained database.
- The chosen Sheet UI needs backend work before it can be built: `services/email_verification.py:82` returns a boolean `verified`, so Verified/Likely/Risky labels, checked-at dates and a launch estimate do not exist yet (CEO review C2). Build the UI only after the conversations.
- Scraper private-address guard before Quarry runs on any server (see `TODOS.md`).

## The Assignment
Before writing any code: list 10 people you can reach who build B2B lead lists today (agency owners, freelance SDRs, founders doing their own outbound). Message 5 of them this week asking for a 20-minute call. In each call, ask them to show you how they built their last list, and write down the tools, hours and cost. Do not demo Quarry. Goal for the week: 3 calls booked and at least 1 done.

- You asked for a research check on whether the lead flow is "correct/not" before anything else. That is a good instinct about trust and standards, and the audit and its fixes show you act on findings.
- The visible work (seven palettes, hardened auth, a security audit) shows taste and follow-through. The gap is the other direction: getting out of the code and in front of a person.
